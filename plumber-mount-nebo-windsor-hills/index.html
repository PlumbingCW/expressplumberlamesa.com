The area

A hillside tract with public stairs

Mount Nebo rises to about 820 feet in La Mesa, and the Windsor Hills subdivision covers part of the mountain. The streets climb the slope, and public stair trails connect them.

The San Diego Reader counts 489 steps in seven segments. The first two short sections, between Fairview Avenue and Vista Drive, date to about 1912 or 1913. Historian James Newland says the rest date to about 1927 as part of the Windsor Hills subdivision.

Newland also says the area was once considered a high-end suburban development, in the same class as Talmadge and Kensington. The San Diego Architectural Foundation notes that stairways like these were common in early twentieth century hillside subdivisions.

Older tracts

What age and slope mean for a visit

We cannot say what is inside any one house here. Pipes, water heaters and drains of different ages often sit side by side once a home has been remodeled. Age alone does not tell you what is in the wall.

What a hillside does change is access. A steep driveway, a narrow street or a set of steps can decide how a plumber carries tools and parts. That is why we ask about it up front.

The problem area

Say which floor the leak or clog is on and whether the space is a closet, crawl space or garage.

Urgent

Water where it should not be? Do this first

Waiting for a plumber feels long when water is moving. These steps limit the damage and keep everyone safe.

  • Find the main shutoff valve and turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • For one leaking fixture, close the small stop valve on its supply line under the sink or behind the toilet.
  • If a drain is backing up, stop using sinks, tubs, washers and dishwashers.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area, and keep the power off near standing water.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call San Diego Gas and Electric or 911 before you call a plumber.

Services

Plumbing work for older hillside homes

When a tank is old or leaking, water heater replacement is the usual answer. California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement, and hillside homes are a good reason to ask the plumber to check yours.

If you suspect a drain or sewer problem, a camera inspection shows what is actually in the line. For a damp yard or a high water bill, water line repair covers the pipe from the meter to the house.

Water

Hard water on the hill

Water in La Mesa comes from the Helix Water District. Helix reported average hardness of 206 mg/L, or 12.0 grains per gallon, for 2025, with a range of 155 to 250 mg/L. On the common USGS scale that counts as very hard. Blends can differ by street, so check your own tap or ask Helix.

Hard water leaves white scale on faucets and shower heads and can build up inside a water heater tank. If that sounds familiar, water softener and filtration options are worth a look.
