La Mesa water

What very hard water means for a La Mesa home

A water softener is a tank of resin beads that swap calcium and magnesium in your water for sodium or potassium, which stops those minerals from forming scale. Water softener installation in La Mesa, CA starts with the number that matters, and Helix Water District gave it in its 2025 report: an average of 206 mg/L as calcium carbonate, or 12.0 grains per gallon.

Helix's reported range ran from 9.1 to 14.6 grains per gallon, and the common scale calls anything above 180 mg/L very hard. That means white crust on shower doors, a tank heater that fills with sediment, and spotty dishes. Blends can vary by street, so test your own tap with a hardness kit or ask Helix before you size a system.

Helix also reported that it disinfects with chloramines. A softener does not remove them, so if taste or smell is your concern, you need a different tool, which this page covers below.

Pick the right tool

Softener, filter, reverse osmosis or scale preventer

These are four different devices that solve different problems. Many homes need one, and some need two.

OptionWhat it doesWhat it does not do
Salt-based softenerRemoves hardness minerals from every tap.Does not filter chlorine, chloramine, lead or bacteria.
Carbon filterReduces taste, odor and some chemicals.Does not remove hardness.
Reverse osmosisFilters drinking water at one tap down to very small particles.Too slow for whole-house use, and wastes some water.
Scale preventerChanges how minerals form crystals.Does not remove hardness, so results vary.
Whole-house sediment filter.Catches sand and rust particles.Does not soften or remove dissolved minerals.

Softening does not purify water. If you want cleaner drinking water, add a carbon filter or reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap.

Sizing

How to size a softener for your household

  1. Measure the hardness

    Use Helix's reported 12.0 grains per gallon as a starting point, then test your own tap.

  2. Estimate daily use

    Count people and multiply by about 75 gallons each per day. A family of four lands near 300 gallons.

  3. Multiply to grains per day

    Gallons times hardness gives grains. For 300 gallons at 12 grains, that is about 3,600 grains per day.

  4. Pick a regeneration interval

    Over a week that is about 25,000 grains. A 32,000-grain unit gives some margin, so it can regenerate every week or so.

  5. Check the space

    A softener needs a drain, a power outlet, a bypass valve, room for a brine tank and access for salt.

Sodium or potassium: which regenerant

The resin works the same way with either. The choice comes down to taste, diet and budget.

Salt (sodium chloride)

  • Widely sold and easy to find.
  • Lower cost per bag.
  • Adds a small amount of sodium to softened water.
  • Fine for most households.

Potassium chloride

  • Replaces sodium with potassium.
  • Better for low-sodium diets, if your doctor advises it.
  • Costs more and may dissolve more slowly.
  • Check that your unit is rated for it.
Heaters and fixtures

What softened water does for tank and tankless heaters

Scale insulates the bottom of a tank, so the burner runs longer to heat the water above it. Sediment also rattles and pops as it traps steam bubbles. Softened water slows that build-up, so the heater runs more quietly and the elements stay cleaner.

A tankless unit has narrow passages that scale clogs easily. It is the type of heater most sensitive to hardness, and manufacturers often recommend regular descaling. If you are planning a tankless water heater, a softener ahead of it protects the heat exchanger, and your manual may specify a hardness limit.

Faucet aerators, cartridges and showerheads clog less often too. If you have already seen spotty chrome and slow flow, fixture repairs tend to come back until the hardness is treated.

Regeneration

How a softener cleans itself

When the resin is saturated with hardness, the control valve starts a regeneration cycle. It backwashes the bed, draws strong brine from the salt tank through the resin, rinses the brine out, and refills the brine tank. The drain line sends the wastewater out, usually to a standpipe or floor drain with an air gap.

Timers regenerate on a schedule, while meter-based valves count gallons and regenerate only when needed. Meter valves waste less salt and water, so we prefer them for households with changing schedules. Run a faucet for a minute after a cycle if you notice a salty taste, and tell us if it persists.

When a unit stops working

Servicing a failing softener

If your softener is old or working poorly, one of these is usually the cause. We repair many brands, and we tell you honestly if the unit is past saving.

Error codes or a blank screen

Controllers fail from power surges, dead backup batteries, or loose turbine sensors. We read the code and test the board and motor before replacing anything.

Salt mushing

A sludge at the bottom of the brine tank blocks the brine well. We pump the tank, clean it, and replace the grid if damaged.

Salt bridging

A hard crust forms above an empty cavity. The salt looks full, but it is not dissolving. Break the crust and check the humidity and salt type.

Exhausted resin

Resin that has aged or been hit with chlorine loses capacity. If hardness appears at the tap right after regeneration, the beads may be done.

Clogged injector

A tiny nozzle draws the brine. Sand or iron deposits block it, and the softener never refreshes.

Water conditioners

Water conditioner repair on older units

Many homes have an older water conditioner that was installed before the current owner arrived, or one that got damaged during a remodel when a drain or brine line was cut. We service these units, replace broken valves and lines, and tell you if a new softener would cost less than another repair.

What goes into the price of a softener job

Softener work varies a lot with the equipment and the house. You get a clear price before work starts, based on the following.

System size

Grain capacity, resin volume and tank size set the equipment cost.

Installation location

A garage with a drain is simple. An interior closet with no drain nearby needs more piping.

Add-on filtration

Carbon filters, sediment filters and reverse osmosis units are separate equipment and labor.

Existing plumbing

Corroded galvanized lines or old shutoffs may need replacement before the new unit goes in. See installing new plumbing for related work.

Repair versus replace

A valve rebuild or injector cleaning is smaller than a new unit. We compare both.

Homeowner policies generally do not cover water treatment equipment as an upgrade. If a failed unit floods a garage, ask your insurer whether sudden water damage is covered.
