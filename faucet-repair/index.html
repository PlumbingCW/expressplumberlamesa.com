The basics

Faucet and fixture repair in La Mesa, CA, from a drip to a failed valve

Faucet repair in La Mesa, CA is the work of stopping a leak or restoring flow at a sink, tub or shower. The fix is usually the worn part inside the fixture or the valve feeding it. A steady stream you cannot stop is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is open for exactly that call.

Most fixture calls start small. A drip under the spout, a handle that has gone stiff, or a trickle where there used to be a strong flow. Each one has a specific cause inside the faucet body, and knowing which one saves you from buying the wrong replacement.

A faucet that drips once a second can waste thousands of gallons in a year. That shows up on a water bill long before it ruins a cabinet.

Urgent

A valve that will not close and water running right now

If a shutoff snaps, a stem spins without stopping flow, or a supply line sprays under a sink, you need to cut water upstream before you do anything else. We take these calls after hours and keep same-day slots open when we can.

Shutting down

What to shut off when a valve will not close

  1. The fixture stop

    This is the small oval-handled valve on the supply line under a sink. Turn clockwise gently. Old stops can freeze with mineral build-up, so never force them with pliers.

  2. The room or branch valve

    Some homes have a valve at a bathroom or the kitchen. If you see one on the pipe in a basement or garage, try it before the main.

  3. The main house valve

    This cuts everything. A ball valve closes with a quarter turn of the lever. A round gate valve needs several full turns clockwise.

  4. The water heater

    If hot water is the one running, close the cold inlet on top of the tank. Turn off gas or power to the heater too, so the element or burner does not run on an empty tank.

  5. The street-side valve

    If the main will not close, the utility meter box has a shutoff. Only the water provider should operate it, so call us and we will talk you through your options.

What is inside the handle

Cartridge, ceramic disc, ball or washer: which faucet do you have?

The name on the spout tells you less than the mechanism inside the body. Here is how the common designs fail.

Faucet typeHow it wears outUsual repair
CartridgeSeals harden, handle drips or runs hot and cold together.Replace the cartridge with the same model.
Ceramic discSeals under the discs wear, or scale scratches the discs.New disc cylinder and seals.
BallRubber seats and springs fail, leaking around the base.Rebuild kit with seats, springs and O-rings.
CompressionRubber washer flattens, drip at the spout when closed.New washers and seats.
Single-handle kitchen pull-out.Hose or spray head leaks, handle loosens.Replace hose, O-rings or the faucet.

Cartridge and ceramic disc fixtures often feel smoother, but their parts are brand specific. A match from the wrong brand can leak within a week, so we confirm the model before ordering.

Local water

Why aerators and handles clog so fast with hard water

Helix reported an average of 12.0 grains per gallon in 2025, which counts as very hard on the common scale. Your own street may differ, so test your tap or ask Helix. At that hardness, calcium crusts the screen in an aerator, and flow drops until the stream sprays sideways.

To clear an aerator, unscrew it from the spout end, soak the screen in white vinegar for a few hours, and brush off the flakes. If scale returns every few months, a treatment system for the whole house, such as a softener or conditioner, takes the load off every fixture and the water heater at once.

Scale also scratches the faces of ceramic discs and gums up cartridges. If your handle has gone gritty or hard to turn, the cartridge is likely the place to look.

Supply valves

When the angle stop or shutoff valve itself is broken

An angle stop is the small valve between the wall and the supply line that lets you cut water to one fixture. It sits unused for years and then fails when you finally turn it. Corroded stems, cracked bodies and weeping packing nuts are the usual signs.

A leaking stop is not a fix-it-with-tape job. We close the water above it, cut out the old valve and sweat or compress-fit a new quarter-turn stop onto the pipe. A quarter-turn ball valve takes much less force than the old multi-turn type, which is why we recommend it.

If the leak is really a pipe that split behind the valve, or the pipe is galvanized steel that crumbles when touched, the repair reaches further into the wall. Homes with that old piping may call for replacing worn supply lines instead of a patch.

Showers and tubs

Shower and tub fixtures we repair in the same visit

A showerhead that drips or sprays unevenly usually has a worn washer or clogged nozzles. Unscrew it, soak it in vinegar, and wrap new plumber tape on the arm threads before reinstalling. If the leak continues from the wall arm, the shower valve behind the tile needs attention.

A bathtub faucet that drips, or a handle that spins without gripping, is generally a worn stem or stripped handle splines. We replace the stem or cartridge, install a new handle, and test the diverter that sends water up to the shower.

Cartridge replacement is the most common shower valve repair. We shut off water, remove the handle and trim, pull the old cartridge and fit the matching one. If the valve body is cracked or behind a tile wall without an access panel, we explain your options before opening anything.

Repair the faucet or replace it

There is no rule that says every dripping faucet needs a new one. These points help you decide.

Repair makes sense when

  • The finish is good and the body is not pitted.
  • Parts for the brand are easy to get.
  • Only one leak point, such as a seal or cartridge.
  • The faucet is less than about ten years old.

Replace makes sense when

  • The body is corroded or cracked.
  • Handles are loose and the base wobbles.
  • Parts are discontinued.
  • You want a different style or a pull-out spray.

What drives the price of a faucet repair

A cartridge swap and a full fixture replacement are different jobs. We give you a clear price before work starts, shaped by these factors.

Parts versus new fixture

A cartridge or washer is a minor part. A new faucet adds the unit itself, and brand and finish change that quite a lot.

Valve condition

A stuck or leaking angle stop adds a new valve to the visit, which is cheaper than a second trip.

Access

Under a cabinet full of storage is easy. Behind a tub wall or tile with no access panel takes longer.

Pipe type

Copper, PEX and old galvanized steel behave differently when we cut and reconnect.

Time of day

After-hours emergency calls cost more than a scheduled daytime repair, and we explain that when you call.

Homeowner policies often cover sudden water damage from a failed supply line or burst valve, but often not slow drips or worn parts. Ask your insurer, and photograph the area before we start.
