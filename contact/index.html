What to tell the dispatcher

A short, clear description saves time. Have these five things ready before you dial.

What you see

Say whether it is water, sewage, steam or a dead appliance, and where in the house it shows up.

Street address

Give the street, nearest cross street and unit number. A PO Box ZIP like 91943 or 91944 does not locate the building.

Can you shut it off

Say whether the main valve or a fixture stop valve is closed, and whether the water has stopped.

Any danger

Mention gas smell, water near outlets, a sagging ceiling or anyone who cannot leave the house.

A callback number

Give the number you will answer, in case the call drops. Say if you will be home or if a neighbor will meet the plumber.

What to do first while you phone

Leaks come first. Close the main shutoff valve, then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines. If one fixture is the source, close its small stop valve under the sink or behind the toilet and leave the rest of the house running.

For a backup, stop using water everywhere. Do not flush, run the washer or use the dishwasher. Keep children and pets out of any area where sewage has surfaced, and open windows if the smell is strong.

Gas first

If you smell gas, leave the building before you do anything else. Call San Diego Gas and Electric or 911 from outside. Then call the plumbing line.

After you hang up

Dispatch matches a licensed plumber to your problem and offers a same-day appointment when one is available. The plumber states a clear price before work starts. Nothing begins until you agree to it.

If you want to confirm the plumber's credentials, look up the license at cslb.ca.gov. For help picking the right service before you call, read the services overview or check the service area page.
