Main Water Shutoff Valve: Where It Is and How to Use It

The main water shutoff valve is the one handle that stops all water entering your house. Look near where the supply pipe comes through the wall or slab, often by the water heater, in a garage or at the front of the house. Turn a lever a quarter turn, or turn a round wheel clockwise until it stops.

Do this first

Finding the valve that stops every leak

Knowing where the main water shutoff valve is in your La Mesa, CA home is the difference between a mop-up and a flooded floor. The valve is a manual device on your supply pipe, usually just inside the house where the line enters. Its only job is to close that pipe completely.

Find it today, not during a leak. Walk the front wall of the house, the garage, the utility closet and the area around the water heater. Tie a bright tag to the handle and tell everyone in the home where it is.

Where to look

Usual hiding places

Garage wall

Common in homes with a water heater in the garage. The pipe comes through the wall and the valve sits on it, low or about waist high.

Utility closet or basement wall

Look for a pipe that turns up from the floor next to a pressure regulator or a small bronze valve.

Front of the house

Look at the wall facing the street. A hose bib nearby often sits on the same supply line, so the valve may be a few feet away.

Crawlspace or access panel

Some homes have the valve behind a small hatch. Check closets and hallway panels near the front.

Outside in a ground box

A plastic or concrete box in the yard may hold a second valve on your side of the meter. See the meter section below before touching anything in it.

The fast way

Shutting off the water in under a minute

  1. Walk straight to the valve

    Do not stop to mop. Every second the pipe stays open adds water, so go straight to the main valve.

  2. Close it fully

    Turn a lever until it sits crosswise to the pipe. Turn a wheel clockwise until it stops, and do not force it past that point.

  3. Open a low tap and a high tap

    Open one faucet at the lowest level of the house and one at the highest. This drains the pipes and relieves pressure.

  4. Turn off the water heater

    With no water entering, a gas heater can overheat and an electric element can burn out. Set gas to pilot and switch off the electric breaker.

  5. Photograph the damage and call

    Take quick photos for your insurer. Then call us on (619) 927-0312 if a pipe has burst or you cannot stop the flow.

Ball valve or gate valve

The handle tells you which type you have, and each type fails differently.

Ball valve (lever handle)

  • Quarter turn from open to closed.
  • Lever lines up with the pipe when open.
  • Seals tightly and rarely sticks.
  • Common on newer installations and replacements.
  • Easy to test: it moves smoothly or it does not.

Gate valve (round wheel)

  • Needs several full turns to close.
  • Wheel handle, often with a brass body.
  • Older style that can seize or leak at the stem.
  • Stem can snap if forced.
  • Often replaced with a ball valve when it fails.
The meter

Your valve versus the utility's meter valve

There are usually two valves in the system. Yours is on the house side of the meter, and the meter valve belongs to the water utility. You should never operate the utility's valve. It is not part of your plumbing, and a utility valve is not meant to be turned by homeowners.

If your own valve will not close, or you cannot find one, stop and call the utility so they can close their side. Then call a plumber. A seized or missing house valve is a repair we can make, and it is best done before an emergency rather than during one.

Test it

Test the valve once a year. Put a bucket nearby in case it weeps.

Smaller stops

Fixture stops and the water heater valve

Every sink, toilet and appliance should have its own shutoff, often called an angle stop. Closing the stop under a leaking faucet or behind a running toilet keeps the rest of the house working. Turn them clockwise until snug, then open them again once a year so they do not seize.

The water heater has a cold inlet valve of its own. Close it if the tank is leaking or you plan to drain it, then turn the gas control to pilot or flip the breaker so the unit is not heating an empty tank.

When a pipe breaks

If the leak keeps going after you close the valve

If water keeps flowing after the main is closed, the pipe may be fed from another source or the valve may not be sealing. Treat it as an active leak. Cut power to anything near the water, move valuables and call right away. A burst pipe that is flooding a wall or ceiling is the textbook case for our 24/7 emergency line.

If the leak is outside, between the meter and the house, the underground line may need attention. That is a job for a leaking water service line repair, and it needs the utility and a plumber working together.

Local note

What La Mesa homeowners should know

Helix Water District serves La Mesa, El Cajon, Lemon Grove and unincorporated areas including Spring Valley and Mt. Helix. That means the meter on your street belongs to Helix, not to you. If your own shutoff valve will not close, call Helix so they can close the meter valve, and then arrange a plumber to replace yours.

Do not try to turn the meter valve yourself with a wrench. It is utility property, and the correct move is a phone call. Keep the number for Helix saved next to your own valve location so you are ready in a hurry.
