All Your Drains Are Clogged at Once: What It Means and What to Do

When every drain in a La Mesa, CA house is slow or backing up at the same time, the blockage is almost always in the main sewer line, not in the individual sink or tub. Stop running water, find the cleanout, and call a plumber, because the water you use has nowhere to go and may come up in the lowest fixture.

By the La Mesa Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026

Call (619) 927-0312

One clog or a main line problem?

A single clogged sink is a branch problem. The blockage sits between that fixture and the point where its pipe joins the larger line. When tubs, toilets, sinks and the washer all misbehave together, the blockage is past the point where those branches meet. That is the main line, the pipe that carries everything out of the house toward the street.

This matters because the fix is different. A branch clog often clears with a plunger or a short cable. A main line clog needs a longer cable or a jet, and sometimes a camera to see why the pipe stopped moving.

Branch line clog compared with main line clog

Branch line clog

  • One fixture or one bathroom is slow.
  • Other drains run fine.
  • Plunger or short snake often works.
  • Clog is usually hair, soap or food.

Main line clog

  • Several fixtures slow or backed up.
  • Gurgling in a toilet or tub after a drain runs.
  • Water rises at the lowest drain.
  • Roots, grease or a damaged pipe are common causes.
Warning signs

Signs the blockage is in the main sewer line

Lowest drain backs up first

Water from the washer or an upstairs shower shows up in a floor drain, shower or tub on the lowest level.

Toilet bubbles when another drain runs

Air pushed ahead of water has to escape. Bubbling in a toilet when you run the sink points to a blocked line.

Several fixtures slow together

If a kitchen sink, tub and toilet all slow within the same day, the shared pipe is the common point.

Smell of sewage near the cleanout or a drain

A blocked line can push sewer gas back into the house. Treat a strong smell as a reason to call.

Clogs that return after a quick fix

A plunger clears a drain for a day, then the water slows again. That is a sign the main line is not draining freely.

Right now

What to do the moment every drain is slow

  1. Stop all water use

    No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher, no showers. Every gallon you send down adds to the backup.

  2. Find your cleanout

    Most homes have a capped pipe, often outside near the foundation or at the edge of the yard, where the main line leaves the house. Look for a round plastic or metal cap.

  3. Look, do not open it blindly

    If water stands in the cleanout or is near the top, the line is full. Opening it can release sewage. Wear gloves and eye protection, and have a bucket ready.

  4. Protect floors and belongings

    Lift rugs, towels and boxes away from low drains.

  5. Call a plumber

    Tell us which fixtures are affected, which is lowest, and whether you have found the cleanout. That helps us bring the right cable or jetter.

Use bottled water or a neighbor's facilities until the line is cleared. Chemical drain cleaners are a poor choice here. They can sit in a standing line and splash on whoever opens it later.

Why a main line clogs

Most main line stoppages come from tree roots entering through joints, grease and wipes that build up on rough pipe walls, or a broken or sagging pipe that holds water. Older clay or cast iron lines are more likely to have offset joints than newer plastic ones. Without a camera, you cannot tell which cause you have.

A plumber will usually start with a cable to open the line and then run a camera to see the pipe. If the clog returns often or the camera shows damage, the next step may be a main sewer line cleaning with a heavy cutter or jet or a repair.

When to call and what we bring

Call right away if sewage is coming up into a tub, shower or floor drain, if more than two fixtures are affected, or if the water level in the cleanout does not drop. We are open 24/7 for sewer emergencies. For the clog itself, our crews use drain cleaning with cables and jets and a camera to confirm the line is clear.

We serve homes in Spring Valley and El Cajon as well as La Mesa. Ask about written pricing at the start of the call. The real number is shared as a written price before work starts, and it depends on line length, access to the cleanout, the clog type and whether a camera inspection is needed.

Prevent a repeat

Once the line is open, keep grease out of the kitchen drain, flush only toilet paper, and have a camera look at the pipe if the stoppage was your second in a year. Roots return unless the pipe is sealed or cut back on a schedule.

What a plumber does on a main line call

The visit starts with a look at the cleanout and the lowest drain. A cable machine opens the line, and the plumber notes what comes back: roots, grease, paper or sand. Then a camera follows the line to the street connection to check for cracks, sags and offset joints.

You get a plain report on what was found and whether the line needs cleaning on a schedule or repair. Ask to see the camera footage. If the pipe is damaged, you should see where, at what depth, and why cleaning alone will not hold.

Safety when sewage backs up

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and pets out of any wet area, wear rubber gloves, and wash your hands well afterward. Do not touch electrical outlets or cords near standing water. Turn off power to a flooded room only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.

Soaked drywall, carpet and padding should be pulled out and dried quickly. Take photos for your insurance claim before you clean. Homeowner policies often cover sudden and accidental events but often not gradual damage, so ask your insurer.

Local note

Backups during rainy months in La Mesa

La Mesa gets about 13 inches of rain a year, and most of it falls from November through March. If a backup happens during or right after a storm, check your cleanout first. If water stands in it or if neighbors also report slow drains, call the City of La Mesa at 619-463-6611 to ask whether the street main could be affected.

Ask the City which part of the sewer line is public and which is yours. That tells you who has to clear the blockage, and it keeps you from paying for work on the wrong side of the property line.
