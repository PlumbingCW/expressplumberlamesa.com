How Long a Tankless Water Heater Lasts in La Mesa, CA

A well-kept tankless water heater in La Mesa, CA commonly lasts about 15 to 20 years, which is longer than most tank models. Hard water scale on the heat exchanger is the biggest thing that cuts that number short. Flushing the unit on a schedule, keeping the inlet filter clean and fixing small faults early are what move a tankless heater toward the high end of its range.

The short answer

The realistic lifespan range and why it varies so much

Manufacturers usually describe a tankless heater as a 20-year product when it gets regular care. In practice, units that are never flushed in hard-water areas can fail well before that. A neglected heater may need a new heat exchanger or a full replacement in its first decade.

The reason is simple. A tank heater stores water and wears out slowly from rust. A tankless unit heats water on demand through a narrow metal exchanger, and anything that coats or clogs that exchanger forces it to work harder and run hotter.

So the age on the label tells you less than the care history. Two identical units installed the same day can end up ten years apart in remaining life.

Wear signs

Warning signs your unit is aging early

Most tankless failures give notice. These are the ones to act on.

Temperature swings

Hot, then lukewarm, then hot again often points to scale on the exchanger or a clogged inlet screen.

Repeated error codes

A code that clears and returns is the heater telling you something is restricted, dirty or failing.

Slower hot water at the tap

A drop in flow can come from a blocked inlet screen, not just a bad pump or valve.

Kettle-like rumbling

Boiling or ticking sounds inside the unit mean water is flashing against scale.

Rising gas use

A scaled exchanger burns more fuel to deliver the same heat.

Do it right

How a flush is done and what to expect

  1. Shut off power and gas

    Turn off the electrical supply, close the gas valve and let the unit cool before touching the service valves.

  2. Close the water isolation valves

    A properly installed unit has hot and cold service valves with ports for hoses. If yours has none, that is a good reason to call.

  3. Connect hoses and a pump

    A small submersible pump circulates a descaling solution, often food-grade vinegar, through the exchanger into a bucket and back.

  4. Circulate for the time in your manual

    Time depends on how scaled the unit is. Heavy scale takes longer or a repeat pass.

  5. Rinse, clean the filter and restart

    Flush the solution out with clean water, pull and rinse the inlet screen, reopen the valves and check for leaks before restoring power.

Flush it or leave it alone

Skipping the flush feels like saving effort. It usually costs more later.

What a regular flush does

  • Removes scale before it hardens.
  • Restores steady hot water temperature.
  • Lets us spot a failing part early.
  • Keeps gas use closer to normal.
  • Builds a service record for resale.

What skipping it risks

  • Scale that vinegar can no longer dissolve.
  • Overheating and nuisance shutdowns.
  • A cracked or restricted heat exchanger.
  • A repair bill that outruns the unit's value.
  • Earlier replacement.

When to repair, when to replace

Repair makes sense when the unit is under about 12 years old, the exchanger is sound and the fault is a sensor, igniter, fan or inlet screen. Replace when the exchanger leaks, the heater is near 20 years or a repair would cost close to a new unit.

If your system is a candidate for retirement, we can walk through options for replacing an aging unit with a new one and size it to the fixtures you actually run at once.

Not sure the real issue is the heater? A cracked fitting or failing valve nearby can mimic a failing exchanger, so we check the water connections before condemning the unit.

Pairing a tankless heater with treatment

A softener removes the minerals that build scale in the first place. For a tankless heater, that can mean fewer flushes and a longer exchanger life. It adds a maintenance item of its own, so the trade is real.

If scale is your main enemy, ask about a softener or filter matched to your water before you buy a premium heater. A cheap unit with treatment may outlast a premium unit with none.

Local note

What La Mesa homeowners should know about hard water

Helix Water District reported that its 2025 water averaged 206 mg/L hardness, which is 12.0 grains per gallon. The reported range ran from 9.1 to 14.6 grains per gallon. On the common USGS scale, anything over 180 mg/L is very hard, so even the low end of that range leaves scale behind inside a heat exchanger.

That is why we treat flushing as a yearly habit, not an occasional fix. Hardness can vary from one street to the next, so check your own tap or ask Helix. If your supply is near the top of the range, move the flush earlier. If the unit has gone more than a year, plan the flush sooner rather than later, and clean the inlet screen at the same visit.

The tie-back is easy to measure: if hot water temperature is steady and the error codes stay quiet after a flush, your interval is about right. If scale returns fast, add treatment.

Sources used: Helix Water District 2025 Annual Water Quality Report. Plumbing work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
