Burst pipes

What a burst pipe repair actually involves

A burst pipe is a split, crack or blown fitting that lets water out faster than any drip. If one has opened up in your La Mesa, CA home, our 24/7 line is open, and the next minute counts for more than anything else on this page. Stop the flow first, then read on while you wait.

Callers describe it in plain terms. Water sprays from a wall, runs behind the fridge in the garage or pours from a cracked elbow. Sometimes an old galvanized pipe simply gives way. This page covers what to do right away, why pipes break here, and how each pipe material gets repaired.

Urgent

A pipe just burst: the first 60 seconds

Water damage compounds quickly, so move first and think second. Our 24/7 plumbers work nights and weekends, and we can stay on the phone while you go through this list.

  • Close the main shutoff on your house plumbing. A lever handle is closed when it sits crosswise to the pipe, and a round wheel turns clockwise.
  • If the leak is near the panel, an outlet or a light fixture, stay out of the water and call 911 if you see sparks. Otherwise switch off the breakers for that area.
  • Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the pipes and take pressure off the break.
  • Turn off the water heater, either the breaker or the gas control set to pilot, so it does not heat an empty tank.
  • Soak up water with towels, a mop or a shop vacuum and move belongings out of the wet zone.
  • Take photos before you clean up.

Causes

Why pipes split open in a mild climate

La Mesa winter highs average 66 to 77 F, so freezing is not the usual cause here. That points your search toward the four causes below.

Corrosion

Pipes thin out from the inside or outside until one weak point opens. Pinholes along a run say the whole run is wearing.

Pressure and surges

High pressure strains every joint. A hard water-hammer bang when a valve shuts can crack an old fitting.

Age and brittleness

Old galvanized steel clogs and rusts through. Aged CPVC can turn brittle and crack at a fitting without warning.

Accidental damage

A nail through a stud, a drywall screw or a trench dug too close can nick a line, which later splits.

Poor joints

A badly soldered or glued fitting can hold for years and then let go all at once.

Cracked and broken pipe

A crack in the pipe or at a fitting: the repair itself

Most breaks are fixed by cutting out the damaged section and splicing in new pipe with the right couplings. We locate the full extent of the crack first, then cut back to sound pipe on both sides. A short clamp over a split can slow the leak, but it is a stopgap, not a lasting repair.

A cracked PVC connector or a split elbow is replaced as a unit, since the fitting is the failure. We then look at the pipe next to it. If it is brittle or heavily corroded, a longer replacement avoids a second break next month.

After the new section is in, we turn the water on slowly and check each joint under pressure. You also get a clear price before any cutting starts.

Where a pipe is galvanized or CPVC and showing more than one failure, ask about replacing the entire run rather than patching.

Smaller leaks

Pinholes, copper joints and cracked drain lines

A small spray or a single dripping spot is a pipe leak, not yet a burst. Do not wait on it. Pinholes tend to widen, and the damage behind a wall keeps building even when the drip looks minor.

Copper is the classic case. A half-inch line may spray fine mist from a pinhole, or an old soldered joint behind a shower valve may let go. We fix it with a cut-in sleeve, a new soldered coupling or a push-fit connector.

Drain pipes crack too, and they leak only when water runs through them. A wet area below a toilet or in the garage points to a cracked drain, and we repair it by cutting out the section and fitting a new one. If the crack is on the line toward the street, it becomes sewer line work and a camera look comes first.

On the visit

What happens once the plumber is at your door

  1. Confirm the water is off and the area is safe

    We check the valve, the electrical risk and where the water is traveling.

  2. Open only what is needed

    A small access cut in drywall or a floor panel exposes the break. We avoid cutting more than the repair needs.

  3. Cut back to sound pipe

    The failed section comes out, and we judge how far the damage extends along the run.

  4. Install, pressure test and check

    New pipe goes in, the line is pressurized, and we look at each joint for weeping.

  5. Advise on drying

    We tell you what should dry on its own and what needs a fan, a dehumidifier or removal.

General repair

Leaks that need a repair but are not an emergency yet

Not every leak is a gusher. A slow drip under a sink, a weeping valve or a damp spot in a closet can wait for a scheduled visit if the water is shut off to that fixture. Close the small stop valve under the sink or at the fixture and keep the rest of the house running.

If you cannot tell where the water comes from, a hidden leak search tells us the spot before any wall is opened. In a condo or rental, tell the property manager or landlord, since the break may be on a shared line.

Insurance

Documenting the break for your insurer

Photograph the break, the wet areas and the damaged belongings before you clean up. Keep the failed pipe section in a bag. If the plumber can say whether the break was sudden or from slow corrosion, ask for that in the written invoice.

Call your insurer soon and ask what they want to see before repairs. Many policies expect you to limit further damage, so shutting the water and drying the area are part of the claim. Keep receipts for fans, hauling and cleanup, and do not throw away soaked materials until the adjuster has looked.

Do this tomorrow

Find your main shutoff now and close it once to be sure it moves. A valve that has not turned in years may seize. Tag it with a bright label so anyone in the house can find it in the dark.

What goes into the price of a burst pipe repair

We give you a written price before work starts. These factors move it.

Pipe material and size

Copper, PEX, CPVC and cast iron each take different parts and labor.

How hard the break is to reach

A pipe in an open garage wall is easier than one inside a finished bathroom wall.

Length of pipe that must be replaced

One split elbow is a short job. A corroded run needs more pipe.

Access and restoration

Opening and patching drywall, tile or flooring is separate from the pipe repair.

Water cleanup and drying

Extraction and drying equipment add cost when water has spread.

Homeowner policies often cover sudden and accidental breaks and the resulting damage, but often not gradual leaks or wear. Your insurer can tell you what applies to your policy.
