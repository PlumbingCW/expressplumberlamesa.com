Heat, rain and scale

Four stress points we check for in El Cajon

El Cajon runs hotter and swings harder than coastal San Diego. The record high was 114 F on September 5, 2020, and the city gets about 11.6 inches of rain a year. Those extremes land on specific parts of your plumbing.

Tank heaters in hot garages

A heater in an unconditioned garage or attic works in a hot box all summer. Look for crusty mineral drips at the relief valve and at the cold inlet, and call before the tank base gets wet.

Supply lines in attics and exterior walls

Braided connectors and plastic fittings age faster in sustained heat. Check them for rust-colored beads, kinks or a damp cabinet floor, and shut the angle stop if one is weeping.

Yard drains after a dry stretch

A yard drain or cleanout that sat dry through a long hot season can fill with leaves and silt. Pour a bucket of water into it before the first storm to see whether it moves.

Scale in fixtures and tanks

Helix reported very hard water, so white crust builds on aerators, showerheads and the bottom of tank heaters. Slow flow or a rumbling tank is usually the first sign.

Water quality

Very hard water and what it does to your equipment

Helix Water District serves El Cajon, and its 2025 report gave an average hardness of 12.0 grains per gallon, with a range from 9.1 to 14.6. On the common USGS scale that is very hard. Blends can vary by street, so check your own tap or ask Helix about your address.

Scale is why a tank heater loses capacity and why a tankless unit that needs descaling throws error codes. Flushing a tank and checking the anode rod once a year slows the buildup. If your fixtures crust over quickly, a water softener or filter is worth pricing out.

Before we arrive

What to do in the first few minutes

Find the main valve

In a house it is often at the front wall or near the meter. In a larger building the shutoff may serve only your unit, so ask the manager which valve is yours.

Cut power near water

A flooded garage in an El Cajon summer often holds a heater, a freezer and extension cords. Switch off those circuits at the panel before you step into standing water.

Clear the work area

Move cars, boxes and patio furniture back from the heater, the cleanout or the wet wall. Photograph the damage for your insurer before you mop.
