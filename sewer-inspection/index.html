The basics

A look inside the pipe before you spend money on it

A sewer camera inspection is a recorded look inside the drain line, made by feeding a waterproof camera on a push rod through a cleanout. For homes in La Mesa, CA it is planned work, and most people book one because of repeat clogs, a home purchase or a line that has never been checked.

The camera sees what a cable can only feel. A cable tells us something is in the way. The camera shows whether that something is grease, roots, a dropped object or a crack, and that difference decides whether you need cleaning, repair or nothing at all.

Ask to go through the footage as it plays, so you can ask questions at each point where the pipe changes.

On screen

What the camera can show you

Roots

Fine threads at a joint or a thick mass across the pipe. Both mean moisture is escaping somewhere.

Cracks and breaks

A dark line along the pipe wall, or a gap where a section has split or fallen away.

Offset joints

Two pipe ends that no longer line up. They catch debris and let roots in.

Sags

A low stretch where water sits instead of flowing. Waste settles there and clogs form.

Scale and buildup

Rough, flaking walls or a coating of grease that narrows the opening.

Material and branches

Whether the pipe is cast iron, clay or plastic, and where other lines join it.

Locating

Finding where the line runs and where the trouble sits

Many callers just need to know where the line goes. The camera head carries a transmitter, and a locator held above ground picks up its signal. That gives the position and depth of the pipe and the distance from the cleanout to any damage. We mark the route with paint or flags.

This matters before you plant a tree, pour a patio or dig a trench. If a repair is needed, it also limits how much ground has to be opened. When there is no cleanout, access may be through a toilet or a roof vent, so mention that when you call.

How an inspection goes

  1. Find the way in

    We locate the cleanout and check its size. If none is there, we choose the next best access point.

  2. Clear the view

    A camera cannot see through packed grease. If the line is heavily blocked, a quick cable pass comes first.

  3. Run the camera

    The camera moves at a slow walking pace and stops at anything unusual. Distance marks on the rod tell us how far each problem sits.

  4. Review the footage

    We replay the key moments, explain what you are seeing and talk through the options. You decide what happens next.

Real estate

If you are buying or selling a house

A general home inspector usually cannot see a buried sewer line, and a clean flush test does not prove the pipe is sound. Buyers use a camera inspection to check for roots, cracks and sags before closing. If the video shows damage, you can raise it with the seller while you still have room to negotiate.

Sellers use one to learn what a buyer's inspector might find. Fixing a short bad section before listing is often simpler than arguing about it afterward, and a clean recording gives a buyer something concrete to review.

Older homes are more likely to have older pipe materials, but age alone proves nothing. Only the video does.

Repeat clogs

When the same drain keeps stopping

After a third clog, cleaning alone stops being a sensible use of your money. A camera looks for the cause, whether that is roots at a joint, a sag where waste settles or a crushed section. If it is plain buildup in sound pipe, a high-pressure water pass that scours the pipe wall may end the cycle.

If the pipe is damaged, jetting could make matters worse, and the better step is a repair. In between sits routine cleaning of the main line on a schedule, which the footage helps you set honestly.

Other tests

What a smoke test is for

A smoke test pushes non-toxic, visible smoke into the sewer line while the ends are closed off. Wherever smoke escapes, there is an opening such as a cracked vent, a missing cleanout cap or a broken connection. It finds leaks a camera may not reach.

Smoke does not show how far a pipe is damaged, so it complements the camera and does not replace it. Not every house needs one. We suggest it when odors point to an opening but the video looks clean.

Next steps

What the findings lead to

If the pipe is sound, you have a baseline recording. Keep it, because a clog two years from now can be compared with this footage to show what changed.

If the video shows buildup or minor roots, cleaning is usually the answer. If it shows a break, a spot repair, lining or replacement of the damaged section is the conversation, and you get the price in writing first. We schedule inspections across La Mesa and in neighboring El Cajon.
