What this service is

Finding a hidden leak without tearing the house apart

Leak detection is the work of locating exactly where water is escaping from a pipe, so the repair targets one spot, not a whole wall. La Mesa Express Plumbing handles it across La Mesa, CA with a 24/7 emergency line for leaks that are active right now. Most callers never use those words. They describe a meter that keeps turning, a stain on the ceiling, a floor that feels warm or a bill that will not come down.

This page follows those symptoms one by one. It also explains how each search method works and where it falls short. For the larger cases there are separate pages on a leak under a concrete slab, the line that runs from the street to your house and a pipe that has split open.

Urgent

Water is running or pouring right now

An active leak does damage by the hour, so treat it as an emergency. Our 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and a plumber can stay on the phone while you do the steps below.

  • Close the main shutoff valve on your own house plumbing. Do not touch the water district's valve at the meter.
  • Switch off breakers for any room with wet walls, wet ceilings or standing water.
  • Put a bucket under a dripping ceiling. If paint is bulging, puncture the lowest point so the water drains into the bucket.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone.
  • Photograph the damage and the meter before you clean anything up.

Check it yourself

The meter test: how to tell if you have a leak at all

  1. Stop every use of water

    Turn off taps, the dishwasher, the washer, the ice maker and any irrigation timer. Make sure nobody flushes during the test.

  2. Read the meter

    Lift the meter box lid and look for the small leak indicator, a triangle or star that spins with even a trickle. Write down the dial numbers. Look only, and do not turn anything.

  3. Wait and compare

    Check again after 15 minutes, then after an hour or two. If the numbers moved or the indicator keeps spinning with every fixture off, water is leaking somewhere.

  4. Close your own main valve and look again

    If the meter stops, the leak is inside your house plumbing. If it keeps moving with your main closed, the leak is on the pipe between the meter and your main valve.

Meter and bill

The meter keeps moving and the water bill keeps creeping up

Helix Water District serves La Mesa and owns the water meter. That means questions about the meter itself, a suspect reading or your billing history go to Helix. Ask them for your usage history and whether their records show flow running through the night. Do not operate the district's shutoff at the meter. Your own main valve is the one to use.

A slow rise over several billing periods usually points to a steady, small leak. A running toilet flapper is the most common culprit and costs little to fix. A buried line or a pinhole in a wall is the harder case. For scale, a leak of one gallon a minute loses 1,440 gallons a day, which shows up on a bill fast.

Once the meter test says water is moving, a plumber narrows it down by closing off sections of the house one at a time. Each section either stops the meter or it does not. That sorting step is free of guesswork and saves the cost of searching the wrong wall.

Ceilings and walls

A stained ceiling, bubbling paint or a soft section of drywall

The stain is rarely directly under the leak. Water runs along framing and pipes before it drips, so a brown ring in the dining room can come from a shower drain in the bathroom above. Look for the room above the stain first, then check what runs through that floor.

A supply line leaks all the time, even at 3 am with nothing in use. A drain line leaks only when a tub, sink or toilet above it is used. Running the upstairs shower while someone watches the ceiling tells you which kind you have.

To confirm the spot, a plumber scans the wall with a moisture meter and a thermal camera. A cold, damp patch shows up clearly on the camera. If the reading is still ambiguous, a small inspection hole costs far less than opening a whole wall. Wet drywall that stays damp for days grows mold, so do not wait on a soft section.

Under the house

The sound of running water under the floor

Many callers describe hearing water run when every tap is off, as if someone were showering beneath the house. That sound is a strong sign of a supply leak in a crawlspace or under a raised floor. Damp insulation, dripping from the subfloor and a musty smell back it up.

Drain leaks hide differently. A leak at the drain below a toilet shows up only after flushing, and the soil under it stays wet long after. Do not go into a wet crawlspace if you see wiring near the water.

Under-floor work is where acoustic listening gear earns its keep, because the pipes are reachable but hard to see. A plumber follows the sound, checks joints and fittings and marks the point. Then the repair is usually a short section, not a whole run of pipe.

Buried lines

Leaks in pipes buried between the meter and the house

A buried line cannot be inspected by eye, so detection is the only way to find the spot. La Mesa gets about 13 inches of rain a year, most of it from November through March. A soft, wet patch in the yard during the dry months, with no sprinkler running nearby, suggests a pressurized pipe is leaking.

Metal pipe carries sound well, while plastic does not. Plastic lines are often found with tracer gas or pressure testing instead. If you suspect this case, see options for repairing or replacing the buried service line.

Sprinklers

Sprinkler zones that run too fast or leave soggy patches

A zone that sprays hard, then dies, or one that leaves a mushy strip along the lawn often has a broken head or a cracked lateral line. A valve that does not close fully also lets water creep through all day, which makes the meter move with the timer off.

We isolate each zone, watch the pressure drop on a gauge and mark the failing section. Heads and valves are quick fixes. A cracked buried lateral takes a bit of digging.

Smaller cases

Pools, spas and the wet spot by the meter box

If you have a pool or spa, a water level that drops faster than evaporation can explain may mean a leak in the shell or plumbing. A simple bucket test compares the pool level with a bucket of water on the steps. Call us for a pipe-side check once the bucket test shows a loss.

A wet spot right at the meter box could be on the district's side of the meter or on your side. Call Helix first about the meter, then call us if the water is coming from your own pipe.

Methods

How the search is done and what each method can miss

No single tool works everywhere. A plumber chooses by pipe material, access and what the symptom suggests.

MethodWhat it finds wellTrade-off
Acoustic listeningPressurized leaks in metal pipe.Less reliable on plastic and in noisy areas.
Thermal imagingHot water leaks and wet patches behind walls.Shows temperature, so cold leaks need confirming.
Moisture meterDamp drywall, wood and subfloor.Reads the surface and does not show the exact pipe.
Tracer gasSmall leaks in plastic and buried lines.Needs the line emptied and gas filled, so it takes longer.
Pressure testingWhich section of line is losing pressure.Narrows to a section but not to a single spot.

Most jobs use two methods together. A pressure test finds the section, and listening or gas finds the point.

Before a repair

Why the cause gets found before any repair is sold

A leak is a symptom. The cause might be a failed fitting, a pipe worn through from corrosion or a line damaged by a dig. Each cause leads to a different repair, so guessing at the fix can mean paying twice.

Ask any plumber for the finding in plain terms: where the leak is, what pipe it is in and why it failed. Then ask for a written price for the repair before work begins. You can check any plumber's license on the California Contractors State License Board site at cslb.ca.gov.

If the same line leaks again and again, patching stops making sense. That is the point to discuss replacing the run, whether you live in La Mesa or in neighboring Lemon Grove.

What changes the cost of finding a leak

The real number depends on the job. These are the things that move it.

How hard the leak is to reach

A pipe behind a bathroom tile wall takes longer than one in an open crawlspace.

Pipe material and depth

Plastic and deep lines need tracer gas or pressure testing, which adds time.

Size of the search area

A single bathroom is a small search. A whole building with several units is a larger one.

Number of methods used

One pass with listening gear costs less than a full sweep with several tools.

Repair and restoration after the find

Detection and repair are priced separately, and drywall or flooring repair is its own work.

Homeowner policies often cover sudden and accidental water damage but often not gradual leaks or wear. Some pay for the cost of opening a wall to reach a covered leak, so ask your insurer before work begins and keep the written finding.
