A phone line for plumbing in La Mesa

La Mesa Express Plumbing is a 24/7 phone line for plumbing help in La Mesa, CA and the towns around it, including El Cajon, Lemon Grove and Spring Valley. You call (619) 927-0312, describe the problem, and dispatch arranges a licensed plumber for the job.

The website exists to help you decide what to say on that call. Each service page explains a problem in plain terms, lists what affects the price and tells you what to do while you wait. The address on file is 9089 Dallas St, La Mesa, CA 91942.

The kinds of plumbing the line handles

Drains and sewers

Clogged sinks, tubs and main lines, plus drain cleaning and sewer line repair when a pipe is damaged.

Water heaters

Tank and tankless units, from repair to replacement and installation.

Leaks and bursts

Hidden leaks, slab leaks and pipes that have split, covered by leak detection and burst pipe repair.

Fixtures

Toilets, faucets and disposals that run, drip, jam or leak under the sink.

Water quality and commercial

Softeners and filters for hard water, plus commercial plumbing for shops, restaurants and offices.

How a call is handled

A dispatcher answers, asks what is wrong and where, and checks whether anyone is in danger. If water is moving, you get shutoff instructions before anything else. If you smell gas, you are told to leave and call San Diego Gas and Electric or 911 first.

Next, a plumber is matched to the job. Same-day appointments are offered when available. The plumber states a clear price before work starts, and you decide whether to go ahead.

What to have ready before you call

A short, clear description saves the most time. Tell the dispatcher which fixture or pipe is involved, whether water is still moving, and whether you have already closed a valve. Mention the street and ZIP code so the call can be matched to a plumber who works your side of town.

If you can, find your main water shutoff before you dial. A burst pipe or a failed supply line is far easier to handle once the water is off. Knowing where the valve is also lets the dispatcher walk you through the next step without guessing.

Safety comes before service

Gas smell

Leave the building and call San Diego Gas and Electric or 911 first. Call us after you are outside and safe.

Water near power

If water has reached outlets, appliances or a panel, stay away and shut off power at the breaker only if the floor around it is dry.

Sewage backup

Keep children and pets out of the area and avoid touching the water. A drain cleaning call can start from the phone.

Hot water leak

A leaking tank can scald. Turn off the gas or power to the unit and keep clear until a plumber looks at it.

Why the service pages talk about hard water

Helix Water District, which supplies La Mesa, El Cajon and Lemon Grove, reported an average hardness of 206 mg/L for 2025. That is very hard water on the common USGS scale. Hardness can differ from street to street, so check your own tap or ask Helix about your address.

In practice, hard water leaves scale inside water heaters, on faucet aerators and in shower heads. That is why our pages on water heater repair and water softener and filtration spend time on flushing, anode checks and treatment options.

Where the line works

The line covers La Mesa ZIP codes 91941 to 91944 and the neighboring towns of El Cajon, Lemon Grove and Spring Valley. Each town page lists its own ZIP codes and the local issues that come up most.

If your address is near a boundary, call and ask. The service area page explains how coverage works, and a dispatcher can confirm it in a few seconds.

Check any plumber's license yourself

You should never have to take a plumber's word for it. The California Contractors State License Board runs a free license check at cslb.ca.gov. Type in the license number or business name and read the status before the work begins.

Do this for every contractor, including the ones you find here. It takes two minutes and tells you whether the license is active. For more on reaching the line, see the contact page, or browse the list of services.

Reference

Standards and sources

The standards behind this site are public. California plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the Contractors State License Board at cslb.ca.gov lets anyone verify a plumber's license.
