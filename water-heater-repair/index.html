Water heater repair

When the hot water fails, the repair has to start tonight

A water heater that floods the garage or goes cold in the middle of the night is an emergency, and our 24/7 line exists for that call. Water heater repair in La Mesa, CA means a licensed plumber finds the failed part, tells you plainly whether it can be fixed, and fixes it when it can. A water heater is an insulated steel tank, a heat source, a thermostat and a few safety parts that all have to work together.

Most repair calls come down to a short list of parts. A thermostat or heating element dies, a pilot or igniter will not stay lit, a relief valve drips, or a drain valve weeps. Every one of those is a part swap. A tank wall that has rusted through is not.

Gas, electric and heat pump units fail in different places, and the sections below show what we check first on each. If you are not sure which kind you own, read the data plate on the side of the tank. It lists the fuel, the capacity and the model number.

Urgent

Water heater leaking or flooding right now

Standing water around a tank, a relief valve that is roaring, or a unit too hot to touch needs attention tonight. Call and a dispatcher will line up a visit as soon as the schedule allows, including after-hours and weekend calls. While you wait, the steps below limit the damage.

  • Close the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the heater. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • On a gas unit, set the gas control to off. On an electric unit, switch off its breaker.
  • Stay out of standing water if the heater is electric and the breaker is still on.
  • Move boxes, rugs and cardboard off the wet floor, and wet-vac or mop the water toward a drain or the door.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.
  • Photograph the leak, the tank and the data plate before you clean up, for your insurer.

Water heater leak

Base, fittings or relief valve: reading the puddle

Where the water shows up tells you most of what you need to know. Dry the tank and every fitting with a rag, then watch for ten minutes. The first bead of water usually appears right at the source.

Fittings on top, such as the cold inlet, the hot outlet and the flex connectors, leak at a nut or a soldered joint. A new connector often ends it. A drip from the relief valve or its discharge pipe means the valve is worn or the pressure inside the system is running high. A missing or waterlogged expansion tank is a common cause of that pressure.

Water at the base of the tank is the one that worries us. It can be a loose drain valve, which is repairable. It can also be seepage through a rusted tank bottom, which is not. Tanks do not get patched, because the steel has already failed.

A puddle in the garage is not always the heater. A slow leak in a supply line or under the slab can run along the floor to the corner where the tank sits. If the tank and every fitting stay dry while the floor stays wet, ask for leak detection before you replace anything.

Cold showers

No hot water: the quick checks and the call

Most no-hot-water calls trace to one of four causes. Look for a gas pilot or igniter fault, a tripped breaker or dead element on an electric unit, a failed thermostat, or sediment that slows heating. Check the breaker or the pilot light first, then call if the water stays cold. These are same-day visits when the schedule allows, and the after-hours line stays open when a household has no hot water overnight.

Local water

What La Mesa water does to the inside of a tank

Helix Water District reported an average hardness of 206 mg/L for 2025, which is 12.0 grains per gallon. On the common USGS scale, anything over 180 mg/L is very hard. Blends can vary by street, so check your own tap or ask Helix.

Hard water drops scale inside a tank every time it heats. The scale settles as sediment on the bottom, and a gas burner firing through that layer runs hotter and noisier. An electric element coated with scale burns out sooner. A relief valve with scale on its seat can stick or drip.

The practical answer is a yearly flush and an anode check. Drain a few gallons from the drain valve until the water runs clear, and look at what comes out. Sandy grit means the tank is overdue. The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the tank steel does not, and a rod worn down to the core is a small repair that can add years.

A flush also cuts how much scale your heater has to fight. A whole-house softener or filter lowers the load further, and we can talk through whether it fits your household.

How a repair visit runs

  1. Make it safe

    We close the water, gas or power as needed, check for combustion gas or exposed wiring, and look for leaks above and below the tank.

  2. Find the failed part

    Elements are tested with a meter, thermocouples and igniters are checked on gas units, and heat pump models are read for stored error codes.

  3. Say repair or replace

    You hear what failed, what it takes to fix and what the tank's condition says about its remaining life. The price comes before work starts.

  4. Fix, flush and test

    The part is swapped and the tank is flushed if sediment allows. The discharge pipe, straps and venting get a last look, and the heater runs a full heat cycle before we leave.

Fix it or swap it: what tips the decision

A good repair visit ends with a straight answer on which side your heater falls.

Repair is usually the right call

  • The tank is dry and the leak is at a fitting or valve.
  • A thermostat, element, igniter or thermocouple failed.
  • The unit is still within a normal service life.
  • The anode rod and tank interior check out.

Replacement is usually the right call

  • Water seeps from the tank base or through the jacket.
  • Rust-colored hot water with a worn-out anode rod.
  • Several parts have failed in a short span.
  • The repair would approach the cost of a new unit.
Earthquake straps

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 says water heaters must be braced, anchored or strapped to resist falling or horizontal movement in an earthquake. If your straps are missing or loose, we can fix that during the same visit that repairs the heater.
