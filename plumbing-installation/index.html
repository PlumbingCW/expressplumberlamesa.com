Plumbing installation in La Mesa, CA: what goes in and how it is done

Plumbing installation in La Mesa, CA means setting new fixtures, supply lines, drains, gas piping or backflow devices so they work and pass inspection. It covers a replacement kitchen sink, a new shower, a washer hookup, or a second bathroom added to a home.

Most of our installation calls begin with one question: can it go where I want it? The answer depends on the drain, vent and supply lines already in the house, and on how far those lines have to be moved. We look at the actual pipes before we promise anything.

This page is for planned work. If water is leaking right now, shut off the nearest valve and call us. If a pipe has already split, a burst pipe repair visit is the right call, not an installation.

Common jobs

Installations people call us about most

Sinks, showers and tubs

New kitchen or bathroom sinks, shower valves, tub spouts and new drain connections, including converting old S-traps to P-traps with proper venting.

Appliance hookups

Washer boxes and shutoffs, dishwasher and ice maker lines, and the drain connection that goes with them.

Toilets and disposals

We set new toilets and install garbage disposals, often as part of a kitchen or bath remodel.

Water heaters

New tanks and tankless units are covered on the water heater installation page with venting and gas details.

Hose bibs and outdoor lines

New exterior faucets, frost-free spigots and irrigation tie-ins from the house supply.

Safety first

If you smell gas, leave first and call the gas company

If you smell gas, a rotten-egg odor, or hear a hiss near a gas line, do not switch lights on or off or use your phone inside. Leave the building with everyone in it. From outside, call San Diego Gas and Electric or 911 before you call a plumber.

The gas utility has the equipment to find a leak and shut off service safely. We cannot promise a response time on gas leaks, and neither should anyone else on the phone. Once the utility says the building is safe, we can inspect your piping and make repairs.

Never relight a pilot or tighten a gas fitting yourself if you suspect a leak. A gas company technician or licensed plumber should handle that.

Gas piping

Gas line installation and inspection

A new gas line feeds a stove, dryer, furnace, water heater, fire pit or backyard barbecue. A plumber sizes the pipe for the total load of everything on the line, chooses the material, and routes it to avoid damage. Undersized pipe starves appliances at the farthest end.

After the run is finished, we pressure test the system and check every joint and appliance connection with a leak-detecting solution. Rust, loose flex connectors and corroded fittings are the problems we find most on inspections of older lines.

If you plan a gas appliance move or a barbecue hookup, call us before you buy. The appliance's rating tells us how much gas it needs, and the right shutoff valve location is easier to plan up front. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of La Mesa building staff before work starts.

Any remodel

Rough-in and finish: the two stages of a new plumbing job

  1. Plan the layout

    We confirm where drains, vents and supply lines must run, and check that slopes and distances are workable for your fixtures.

  2. Rough-in

    Pipes go into open walls or floors before drywall and tile cover them. Inspections, if the City asks for them, happen at this stage.

  3. Pressure test

    We hold the supply system under pressure and fill the drains to confirm there are no weeping joints before anything is closed up.

  4. Finish plumbing

    After walls and floors are done, we set the fixtures, connect traps, and open the valves.

  5. Final check

    We run every fixture, check for slow drains and drips, and show you where each shutoff valve is.

Backflow

Backflow testing and prevention

A backflow preventer is a device that stops dirty water from flowing backward into the clean supply. You see them on irrigation lines, fire sprinklers, some commercial plumbing and some home setups. A test checks that its internal check valves hold and that the relief valve opens when it should.

Testing is planned work, not an emergency. Who requires it and how often depends on the device and the property. Ask your water provider or the City of La Mesa which devices need a test and who must do it.

Keep any notice you receive and call with the device's location and its make if you have it. If a test fails, parts such as seals and springs are usually replaced and the device is retested the same visit.

Building new

New construction and remodel plumbing

New construction and larger remodels need a plan before any pipe is cut. We coordinate with builders and general contractors, work around framing and tile schedules, and do the rough-in and finish stages. Bring your plan set and fixture list when you call.

Some jobs are a swap. Others change the layout of the house.

Basements and low rooms

Sump and ejector pumps, if your plan needs one

If you are adding a bathroom or laundry below the level of the sewer line, a sewage ejector pump lifts waste up to the drain. A sump pump removes ground water from a pit. Both are needed only when gravity will not do the job, and your layout decides whether yours is one of those homes. Toilets in that kind of room, covered under toilet installation, may need the pump.

Access

Materials

Existing condition

Inspections and permits
