Definition

The buried line from the meter to your house

A water service line is the underground pipe that brings water from the meter at the street to the main valve at your house. When it leaks in La Mesa, CA, you can lose water, pressure and a chunk of the yard at once. Our 24/7 line is open, and a licensed plumber can walk you through what to shut off.

This work is different from repiping. Repiping replaces the supply pipes inside the house, in the walls, attic and crawlspace. A service line repair or replacement stays outside, between the meter and your front wall. If your leak is inside, our page on replacing the pipes inside your walls covers that job.

Outside line or inside pipes: which job is yours?

The symptoms overlap, so the quickest way to sort them out is to ask where the water is turning up.

Water service line (this page)

  • Wet or sunken ground between the street and the house.
  • Meter keeps moving while your own main valve is closed.
  • Pooling near the foundation with no wall or ceiling damage.
  • Pressure drops across the entire house at once.

Repiping (inside the house)

  • Stains on ceilings and walls.
  • Pinhole leaks at several fixtures.
  • Rusty water from older galvanized pipe.
  • Low flow at one tap or one bathroom.
Who is responsible

Where the district's pipe ends and yours begins

Helix Water District serves La Mesa, El Cajon and Lemon Grove, and it owns the meter. Ask Helix where its responsibility ends and the property owner's begins on your street. That line is not always where people assume, and it decides who pays for which stretch.

Do not operate the district's shutoff at the meter, even if you can reach it. Close the main valve on your side, usually at the house wall or in the garage. If water is leaking before the meter, call Helix. If it is after, call us.

Urgent

A water line emergency: the yard is flooding and the line will not hold

A failing service line can wash out soil and flood a foundation, so treat it as urgent. Our 24/7 emergency line is open overnight and on weekends, and the steps below limit damage while we get there.

  • Close the main valve at your house, not the district's valve at the meter.
  • Turn off irrigation so you are not adding water to a soaked yard.
  • Keep people and vehicles off soft, sunken ground near the leak.
  • Move anything stored against the foundation where water is pooling.
  • Note the meter reading, then look again in an hour so we can see how fast it is moving.

Service line leaks

Meter still running after you shut everything off

The most common sign of a service line leak is a meter that moves while every tap inside is closed. Close your own main valve and look again. If the meter keeps turning, the break is on the buried pipe between the meter and the house.

Other signs are a damp strip across the lawn, a patch of green grass in a dry season or a hiss from near the front wall. Some callers realize the line is simply old and decide to replace it before it fails.

Our work begins with locating the break. We use listening gear, tracer gas or a pressure test, depending on whether the pipe is metal or plastic. Then we explain whether a short repair or a full replacement fits the condition of the pipe. You get a clear price before digging starts.

Underground

Finding a leak you cannot see

A buried leak may not show at the surface for weeks. La Mesa gets about 13 inches of rain a year, mostly from November through March, so a soggy patch in July that sprinklers cannot explain is a strong clue. In winter, rain hides the signal, which makes the meter test more important. For other symptoms, see our page on finding hidden water leaks.

Pressure

Weak flow throughout the house

Low pressure can have several causes besides a broken line. A pressure regulator that has worn out or been set wrong is one. A main valve that was left partly closed after a repair is another, and so is a stuck stop valve at a fixture.

Inside older homes, galvanized steel pipe rusts and narrows until flow drops, mostly at the far fixtures. A leak in the service line takes water before it reaches the house, so pressure and the meter both tell the tale. We check the regulator and valves first because they are quick, then test the line.

We make no claims about the district's supply pressure. We measure what reaches your house with a gauge and compare it to what your fixtures should receive.

Repair the line or replace it?

A repair makes sense when

  • One break shows up in an otherwise sound pipe.
  • The pipe is modern and the failure is a joint.
  • The leak is short and the ground is easy to dig.

Replacement makes sense when

  • The pipe is galvanized, rusted or full of pinholes.
  • The line has leaked more than once.
  • Pressure has fallen and the pipe is old.
  • You plan to build near the line.
New lines

Installing a new line for an addition, garage or second unit

Sometimes the need is a new line rather than a repair. A garage conversion, a detached unit or a relocated meter connection all need a fresh pipe sized for the load. We lay out the route, set the depth, install the pipe and add an accessible shutoff valve at the house.

Permit rules vary, so ask City of La Mesa building staff before work starts. The same goes for a new line that crosses a property boundary. For related fixtures at the end of the line, see our work on setting up new plumbing.

What drives the cost of a service line job

Length and depth of the run

A longer or deeper line takes more digging and pipe.

Repair or full replacement

A spot repair is small. A full line replacement is a bigger project.

Digging method

Trench or trenchless changes both labor and the surface restoration afterward.

What is above the pipe

Lawn, planters, concrete or pavers each have different restoration needs.

Pipe material and valves

Material choice and a new shutoff valve add to the parts list.

Homeowner policies often cover sudden and accidental events but often not gradual leaks, wear or the line itself outside the house. Ask your insurer what applies before digging begins.
