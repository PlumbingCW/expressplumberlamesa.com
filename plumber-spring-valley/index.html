Where Spring Valley sits and who we serve there

Spring Valley is a census-designated place in San Diego County, which means it is an unincorporated community rather than a city. A plumber in Spring Valley works across about 7.4 square miles and 30,998 people at the 2020 census. We are based at 9089 Dallas St in La Mesa, CA, and our 24/7 line covers the community.

The name comes from a natural spring. That history is nice, but the practical point is that the community has no city hall of its own, and that changes who you ask when work needs a permit.

Before work starts

Unincorporated land and permit questions

Because Spring Valley is unincorporated, do not assume the nearest city's rules apply to your house. Ask the County of San Diego or the right permit office before work starts. A water heater swap, a sewer replacement or a repipe can raise the question.

Ask what paperwork is needed, who inspects and whether anything must be open for the inspector. We can tell you what we plan to do, and you or we can bring that to the office. Get the answer before the work, not after.

Water and housing

Hard water and mixed home ages

Helix Water District serves Spring Valley, and its 2025 report gave an average hardness of 12.0 grains per gallon. That is very hard, so tanks, faucets and shower valves pick up scale. Check your own tap or ask Helix, because blends can vary.

If your house is older than about 40 years, ask about the age of the supply pipe and the sewer lateral. Galvanized steel narrows with rust, and clay or cast iron drains crack with roots. A camera look is the quickest way to tell. Newer additions may use PEX or plastic and behave differently.

Practical note

If you are not sure which part of a sewer line belongs to you, ask the County of San Diego before you pay for a repair. We will show you the camera footage to take with you.
