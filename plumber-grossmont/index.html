The area

What people mean by Grossmont

Grossmont is a named area of La Mesa. The name goes back to 1902, when Ed Fletcher and William Gross bought the Villa Caro Ranch and Fletcher named a small peak on the north side of Mt. Helix after Gross. That account comes from the La Mesa History Center.

Today the name appears in many places. Grossmont Center is a shopping center at 5500 Grossmont Center Drive in La Mesa. Grossmont Transit Center is a trolley stop served by the Orange and Green lines. Grossmont High School has an El Cajon address but includes some La Mesa students.

Because the name is so widely used, your street tells us more than the word Grossmont does. We use the street to confirm that you are inside La Mesa.

Homes and shops

Two kinds of calls in one area

The area mixes retail, transit and residential streets. One real estate data site that profiles a nearby area it calls Grossmont Summit says almost all of the homes there are detached single-family houses and most are owner occupied, with many built from 1940 to 1969. Treat that as a general description from a data source, not a survey of any street.

So we plan for two kinds of calls. A homeowner needs a plumber who can work inside a house. A shop tenant needs someone who can sort out what the building owner controls.

Urgent

Water where it should not be? Do this first

Waiting for a plumber feels long when water is moving. These steps limit the damage and keep everyone safe.

  • Find the main shutoff valve and turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • For one leaking fixture, close the small stop valve on its supply line under the sink or behind the toilet.
  • If a drain is backing up, stop using sinks, tubs, washers and dishwashers.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area, and keep the power off near standing water.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call San Diego Gas and Electric or 911 before you call a plumber.

Services

Work for homes and tenants

Shop and office tenants often need commercial plumbing help with restrooms, sinks and drains. At home, a water heater that stopped heating is a common reason to call. California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement, so ask the plumber to check that too.

A damp wall or an odd water bill can point to a hidden leak. Leak detection looks for the source without tearing open the whole wall. If you are adding a fixture, plumbing installation covers new lines and connections.

Water

Hard water around Grossmont

Water in La Mesa comes from the Helix Water District. Helix reported average hardness of 206 mg/L, or 12.0 grains per gallon, for 2025, with a range of 155 to 250 mg/L. On the common USGS scale that counts as very hard. Blends can differ by street, so check your own tap or ask Helix.

Hard water leaves white scale on faucets and shower heads and can build up inside a water heater tank. If that sounds familiar, water softener and filtration options are worth a look.
